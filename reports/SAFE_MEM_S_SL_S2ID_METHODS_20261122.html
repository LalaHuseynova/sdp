Shared machinery

One scoring rule, different scopes

RISK OF A SCOPE

risk = (1 + unsafe) / (2 + unsafe + safe)

unsafe, safe = numbers of unsafe and safe audits in that scope; Beta(1,1) prior, so no evidence gives 1/2.

PENALTY

penalty = max(0, risk − ½)

DECISION

penalty ≥ 0.25 → withhold. Otherwise score − 0.5 × penalty (rank down).

unsafesaferiskpenaltyeffect on memories in the scope
001/20none
102/31/6ranked down only
203/41/4withheld
213/51/10ranked down only
513/41/4withheld
  • Each independent root counts once; only authenticated external sources are penalized.
  • Principal (user’s own) memories are never penalized. A memory audited unsafe is always withheld for that use.
  • R, C, M and S share this rule. They differ only in which scopes are checked.

dynamic/policies.py (weight 0.5, threshold 0.25, Policy.decide) and dynamic/ledger.py (GroupStats.penalty).

Speaker notes

Key consequence: one unsafe audit alone never blocks anything; it only ranks down. Blocking needs two unsafe audits and no safe one in the same scope. One safe audit raises the bar to five unsafe.

Method 1 · S

S: a ladder of evidence gates

S uses the same scoring rule, but a wider scope is only switched on when the audits support it.

GATE 1
Batch

Opens when: ≥ 1 unsafe audit of this contributor in this context and batch.

Then: score the memories of that batch.

GATE 2
Context

Opens when: ≥ 2 unsafe audits of this contributor in this context, spanning ≥ 2 batches.

Then: score the whole context.

GATE 3
Contributor

Opens when: ≥ 4 unsafe audits in total and ≥ 2 contexts that each pass gate 2.

Then: score the contributor everywhere.

An open gate only means the scope is scored. Memories are withheld only when the penalty of an open scope reaches 0.25 (for example, 2 unsafe and 0 safe). S takes the maximum penalty over its open scopes.

dynamic/ledger.py active_scopes (support gates) and dynamic/policies.py (max penalty over active scopes).

Speaker notes

Compared with M, which always checks batch, context and source, S checks a wider scope only after the evidence clears the matching gate.

Method 1 · S

S, step by step

  1. A question arrives; retrieval proposes candidate memories.
  2. If a candidate was itself audited unsafe for this use, it is withheld. If audited safe, it is kept with no penalty.
  3. If it comes from the principal or from an unknown source, there is no source penalty.
  4. Otherwise, find its open scopes (gates 1–3) for its contributor, context and batch.
  5. For each open scope compute the penalty from its unsafe and safe audit counts; take the maximum.
  6. Penalty ≥ 0.25 → withhold. Penalty > 0 → rank down by 0.5 × penalty.
  7. Freed context slots are refilled from the remaining candidates.
WORKED EXAMPLE: LOCALIZED

A · X · batch 1 holds 2 seeds audited unsafe.

Gate 1 open: batch has 2 unsafe, 0 safe → penalty 1/4 → withhold batch 1.

Gate 2 closed: both unsafe audits sit in one batch.

Gate 3 closed: no context passes gate 2.

→ A’s batch 2 facts are kept.

Steps 2–6: Policy.decide. Step 7: internal methods refill freed slots; published defenses filter without refill (protocol v2 settings, plan.md 22 Nov paper check).

Method 1 · S

S: what it does well and where it breaks

Advantages
  • Localized attack: 0% target ASR at 91.0 strict utility, the same utility as Original SAFE-Mem (Table 1).
  • Keeps A’s benign facts in other batches: key-stratum utility +90 points vs C and M (H1).
  • As safe as M under the distributed attack (H2).
  • Transparent rule: every withheld memory has a scope and audit counts behind it.
Limitations
  • Trusts provenance labels. Sybil identities and laundering by innocent relays defeat it (Table 2: 63.5–65.6 target ASR).
  • Audit-aware batch shift: 65.6 target ASR, where C and M reach 0 (Table 7a).
  • Framing harms innocents: innocent utility 52.5 (concentrated) vs 91.7 for Original (Table 8).
  • No advantage for innocents under 1 false-positive audit of 5 (H6 not supported).
Bottom line: S is the right scope when provenance labels are honest and audits land on the attack.

Method 2 · SL

SL: S plus content lineage

Main idea

Provenance cannot see a copy sent by someone else. SL adds one rule on top of S: withhold any stored claim that is near-identical to a claim of a memory audited unsafe.

  1. Run S as usual.
  2. Compare each candidate’s claim with the claims of every root that has an active unsafe audit (local MiniLM vectors).
  3. Cosine ≥ 0.8 → withhold for every use, from any contributor, unless that claim was itself verified safe for this use.
DESIGN CHOICES
  • Principal memories are never matched.
  • It never creates an audit, so it cannot raise anyone’s risk (no framing amplification).
  • Evidence chunks are excluded.
  • tau = 0.8 was chosen after the v1 test results, so SL is not part of the hashed-before-test constants.

workload/lineage.py (DEFAULT_TAU = 0.8, LineagePolicy.decide; docstring).

Method 2 · SL

SL: strengths and limits

Advantages
  • Catches copies across identities: relay exposure 58 → 0 at no utility cost (H5, both models).
  • Same localized result as S: 0% target ASR at 91.0 utility.
  • Cannot frame anyone: it only withholds and never adds audit evidence.
Limitations
  • Only helps when the copy is near-identical to an audited memory. The scored items are different claims, so Sybil and laundering are not fixed (Table 2: SL 65.6–66.7).
  • Paraphrases below cosine 0.8 pass; benign near-duplicates of an audited claim would be withheld.
  • tau chosen after v1 results (not pre-test).
  • Innocent utility under 1 false-positive audit: 86.2 vs 91.6 for S (Table 8).
Bottom line: a simple add-on that fixes relayed copies, but not identity attacks.

Tables 1, 2, 4, 8 of paper_tables/tables.html.

Method 3 · S2-ID

S2-ID: SL plus an identity-blind gate

Main idea

Run SL, then apply MemLineage’s sensitive-action gate to the final context. The gate ignores who sent a memory and only asks is it external?

  1. Run SL (S + lineage) and build the context.
  2. Look up the question’s recorded MemLineage verdict: SENSITIVE or ORDINARY (an unrecorded question raises an error).
  3. SENSITIVE and any memory in the context is non-principal → return an empty context (MemLineage’s Deny).
  4. ORDINARY → the SL context is used unchanged.
SENSITIVE (classifier prompt)

Acting on the answer would send an email or message; send, upload, export, sync or back up data; execute code; query or modify a database; or transfer money.

LABELS

MemLineage’s coarse labels reduce to external ⇒ untrusted, principal ⇒ trusted (tested).

workload/sv2.py (_GatedReader, RecordedVerdicts, docstring); classifier prompt: prompt_mirror/…/sensitive_action/v1.txt.

Method 3 · S2-ID

S2-ID: strengths and limits

Advantages
  • Lowest target ASR of all our methods under adaptive attacks, e.g. Sybil k=4: 46.9 vs 63.5 for S (GPT-4o-mini).
  • H4 supported: −14 points vs S over 5 adaptive cells (GPT-4o-mini), −7 (Haiku), utility difference 0.
  • Keeps S’s localized result: 0% target ASR at 91.0 utility.
Limitations
  • Its gain over S comes from the borrowed gate, not from a new mechanism.
  • Query-level and coarse: on SENSITIVE questions it also withholds legitimate external memory; on ORDINARY questions the gate adds nothing.
  • Depends on an LLM classifier (verdicts recorded from MemLineage’s run).
  • Worst case 46.9 on GPT-4o-mini, above MemLineage’s 43.8 (Table 3).
Bottom line: the most robust of our methods, but its robustness comes from MemLineage’s gate.

Comparison

S2-ID vs MemLineage

MemLineage (published)
  • Labels memory trusted or untrusted by origin; here that reduces to external vs principal.
  • On a SENSITIVE question with untrusted memory in context, it denies the context.
  • Reads no audits and no contributor identity, so it is flat across Sybil and laundering.
  • Filters Original SAFE-Mem’s retrieval, with no refill.
  • Our version is a paper-based adaptation, not upstream code.
S2-ID (ours, hybrid)
  • The same gate, applied after S and lineage.
  • Also uses audits: blocks unaudited poison on ordinary questions too, when the evidence supports it.
  • Localized: 0.0 vs 43.8 target ASR (both 91.0 utility).
  • Sybil and laundering: 45.8–46.9, close to MemLineage’s 43.8, because S’s part is defeated and the gate does the work.

Neither is better everywhere. S2-ID wins clearly when audits carry signal (localized: 0 vs 43.8); MemLineage has the lower worst case on GPT-4o-mini (43.8 vs 46.9).

Table 1 · interpretation

Why the scope decides utility

Why S, SL, S2-ID: 0% at 91
  1. Both seeds of a context sit in A’s batch 1 with the 2 scored items.
  2. Gate 1 opens; the batch has 2 unsafe, 0 safe → penalty 1/4 → the scored items are withheld.
  3. Gates 2 and 3 stay closed, so A’s batch 2 facts are kept.
  4. SL and S2-ID start from S, so they inherit this (the gate also costs no utility here).
Why C and M: 0% at about 66
  1. C checks A’s whole context: 2 unsafe, 0 safe → all of A in X and Y withheld.
  2. M takes the max with the contributor scope: the same outcome here.
  3. The lost points are A’s own benign facts in batch 2: key-stratum utility is about 90 for S and about 0 for C and M (H1: +90).

Caveat: this is the setting S is designed for: oracle audits and attack items concentrated in one batch. Under the distributed attack (seeds in both batches), S, C and M all reach 0% target ASR with utility of about 66 (analysis/per_cell.md, GPT-4o-mini).

Mechanism: ledger.py active_scopes and policies.py; H1 key stratum = "same contributor, same context, other batch" (Table 4).

Key findings

What the results show

1. Localized attack: S, SL and S2-ID reach 0% target ASR with no utility loss (91.0 GPT, 90.7–90.8 Haiku). C and M also reach 0% but drop to about 66.

2. S beats every published defense on the localized attack: −44 vs MemLineage (GPT), −15 vs A-MemGuard (Haiku) (H3).

3. SL removes relayed copies (relay exposure −58) at no utility cost (H5).

4. Provenance manipulation (Sybil k=4, laundering r=1) brings S and SL back to Original’s level.

5. S2-ID lowers adaptive-attack ASR vs S by 14 (GPT) and 7 (Haiku) points (H4), thanks to the borrowed gate.

6. H1–H5 supported and H6 not, on both models: same method ranking. Replication evidence (reused split).

Limitations

What these results do not show

  • Provenance trust: S and SL assume contributor labels are honest; Sybil and laundering break this.
  • Oracle audits are a strong assumption: they always land on attack seeds.
  • Framing: planting poison under innocent names lowers innocents’ utility for S, C and M (Table 8).
  • Audit-aware batch shift defeats S (65.6) but not C or M (0).
  • Reused MPBench v4 split: replication evidence, not confirmatory.
  • Haiku ran only on the 8 pre-registered cells, not all 62 attack cells.
  • S2-ID borrows MemLineage’s gate, which is query-level and needs an LLM classifier; our MemLineage is a paper-based adaptation.
  • SL’s tau = 0.8 was chosen after the v1 test results.

Tables 7a, 8 (GPT-4o-mini only) and the paper_tables Part B note; lineage.py; sv2.py.

Conclusions

Take-aways

S shows that audit evidence can be propagated selectively: in the localized setting it removes the attack and keeps all utility, which C and M cannot.

SL closes the relayed-copy gap with one rule and no utility cost.

S2-ID is our most robust variant under Sybil and laundering, but its gain comes from MemLineage’s borrowed gate, and MemLineage still has the lower worst case on GPT-4o-mini.

Provenance-based propagation is only as strong as its labels. Identity-blind signals and harder audits are the open problems. These are replication results on a reused split; no method here is best everywhere.
